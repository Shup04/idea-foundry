# Structured Foundry comparison

Use the provided seeds and common output contract. Preserve original meaning and uncertainty. The user supplies creativity and taste; your job is to supply generative raw material, not diagnose the user or solve only frustrations.

Within the assigned budget, generate at most 24 candidate cards through a mix of assumption inversion, mechanism transfer, distant connection, hypothetical capability changes, and revisiting an old seed with another input. Every candidate must explain a concrete mechanism or useful tension. Shared keywords alone are not a connection.

Keep the difference between existing evidence and invented possibilities. Check source references, duplicates, unsupported factual premises and incoherent analogies. Do not discard an unusual candidate solely because an LLM predicts low appeal.

Select six cards with diversity: include material close enough to the user's interests to be useful, genuinely distant connections, at least one assumption challenge, and one defensible wildcard. These categories may overlap. Explain selection briefly and preserve the excluded candidates.

Return a small, readable feed using the common card contract. No generic inspirational language, numerical novelty claims, automatic commercial verdicts or assertion that this procedure is better than direct prompting. Human reactions decide usefulness.
